\begin{abstract}
We describe and formalise the \emph{operator layer} of MT-LNN, a
brain-inspired liquid neural network. The layer is a library of small,
self-contained mathematical primitives -- dynamical-systems analysis,
information geometry on the probability simplex, spike-timing plasticity,
$H_0$ persistent homology, Newtonian micro-physics, binaural acoustics,
spatial graphs, stream alignment, experience replay, continual-learning
metrics, and a global-workspace salience detector. The layer obeys a single
strict engineering contract: \textbf{(i)} no operator imports the model
backbone; \textbf{(ii)} every operator carries zero trainable parameters;
\textbf{(iii)} every stateful operator exposes an idempotent \code{reset()};
\textbf{(iv)} every operator computes in an explicit, caller-independent
dtype discipline; and \textbf{(v)} every operator's mathematical behaviour
is pinned by property-based tests. The contract makes the operators a
genuine \emph{algebra}: composable, side-effect-free instruments that can be
attached to the continuous-time liquid-time-constant (LTC) core without
coupling to it. We give exact definitions for each operator, state the
invariants its tests pin, and -- as a matter of scientific hygiene -- draw a
sharp line between the handful of closed-form identities the layer truly
guarantees and the larger set of \emph{property-test-pinned} laws that are
empirically robust but not formally proven. We close with the layer's
honestly admitted limitations: epsilon floors that break exact
scale-invariance, far-field approximations, first-order energy stability,
and an $H_0$-only topology surrogate.
\par\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} liquid neural networks, operator algebra,
property-based testing, information geometry, persistent homology,
spike-timing-dependent plasticity, continual learning, global workspace.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ===================================================================== %
Brain-inspired model engineering tends to accrue heterogeneous mechanisms:
plasticity rules, geometric regularisers, salience gates, replay buffers,
sensorimotor front-ends. Bolted directly into a training loop, such
mechanisms entangle with the backbone, resist testing, and silently drift.
MT-LNN takes the opposite stance. Each mechanism is first distilled into a
\emph{pure mathematical operator} that knows nothing about the model it will
eventually serve. The operators form a layer that sits beside -- never
inside -- the network, and they earn their place by satisfying a uniform
contract that makes their behaviour auditable.

This paper consolidates the mathematics of that layer. The thesis is
deliberately modest and concrete: \emph{a useful brain-inspired system can
be built from a small algebra of zero-parameter, model-decoupled primitives
whose every law is pinned by an executable test}. We are explicit about what
``pinned'' means. Most of the laws below are verified by Hypothesis
property tests that draw inputs from a bounded search space and assert an
invariant within a finite tolerance. That is strong empirical evidence, not
a proof. A minority are genuine closed-form identities. We flag which is
which throughout, and we devote Section~\ref{sec:honesty} to the
distinction.

\paragraph{Scope.} We cover the eleven operator modules and the LTC core
they are designed to instrument. We do \emph{not} claim leaderboard
results; the contribution is a design discipline and its formalisation. We
also do not claim that every operator is currently wired into the training
objective -- several (notably the plasticity and Riemannian operators) are
deliberately kept at the operator layer, available but not yet integrated.

\section{The Layer Contract}\label{sec:contract}
% ===================================================================== %
The operators are not merely a utility grab-bag; they satisfy a contract
that we verified directly against the source (not against docstrings).

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Backbone decoupling.} No operator module imports the model
backbone. The only inter-operator dependency is that the topology module
reuses the spatial module's graph primitives. This is what lets an operator
be reasoned about, and tested, in isolation.
\item \textbf{Zero parameters.} No operator instantiates a trainable
parameter. The stateful operators that advertise a parameter count
(\code{ReservoirBuffer}, \code{ClassBalancedReservoir},
\code{SalienceEventDetector}) hard-return \code{n\_parameters == 0}. The
pure-function operators hold no state at all.
\item \textbf{Resettable state.} Every stateful operator exposes a
\code{reset()} that returns it to its constructed state (cleared buffers,
re-seeded RNG, neutral baseline). Pure-function operators need none.
\item \textbf{Dtype discipline.} Operators compute in an explicit dtype
chosen for the task -- the continual-learning metrics, for instance, compute
in \code{float64} regardless of caller dtype so that reported scores are
citation-exact. Property tests build their own \code{float64} tensors and
never mutate the global default, so they cannot perturb \code{float32} model
tests sharing the process.
\item \textbf{Property-pinned behaviour.} Every operator ships a test that
asserts its mathematical invariants over randomised inputs.
\end{enumerate}

The contract is what upgrades a collection of functions into an
\emph{algebra}: because the pieces are pure (or explicitly resettable),
parameter-free, and decoupled, they compose without hidden interactions, and
their composition can be tested as freely as the parts.

\section{The Continuous-Time Core Being Instrumented}\label{sec:ltc}
% ===================================================================== %
The operators exist to measure, regularise, and feed a liquid core. Unlike
the operators, the core \emph{is} a parametric module; it is the object of
study, not part of the algebra.

The production core, \code{VectorizedMultiScaleResonance}, evaluates the
closed-form liquid-time-constant (LTC) update across $P$ protofilaments and
$S$ timescales simultaneously. For protofilament $p$, scale $s$, with fixed
step $dt$ and a learned time constant $\tau_{p,s}>0$,
\begin{align}
\text{decay}_{p,s} &= e^{-dt/\tau_{p,s}}, &
A^{p,s}_{b,t} &= \sigma\!\big(W^{p,s}_{\text{in}}\,x_{b,t,p} + b^{p,s}_{\text{in}}\big),\\
h^{p,s}_{\text{new}} &= h_{\text{prev}}\,\text{decay}_{p,s}
   + A^{p,s}_{b,t}\,(1-\text{decay}_{p,s}),
\end{align}
followed by a cross-scale blend $h_{b,t,p} = \sum_s w_{p,s}\,h^{p,s}_{\text{new}}$
with $w_{p,\cdot} = \mathrm{softmax}(\text{blend\_weights}_{p,\cdot})$, and
$\tau = \mathrm{clamp}(\mathrm{softplus}(\log\tau)+\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max})$.
The legacy single-protofilament \code{ProtofilamentLTC} is the same closed
form with a scalar $\tau$.

Algebraically, each step is a discrete \emph{affine contraction toward a
state-dependent equilibrium},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ltc-affine}
h_t = e^{-dt/\tau}\,h_{t-1} + (1-e^{-dt/\tau})\,A_t,
\end{equation}
with fixed $dt$. This is exactly the structure the dynamical operators of
Section~\ref{sec:attractor} are built to analyse, and the fixed $dt$ is the
grid onto which the ingest operators of Section~\ref{sec:ingest} resample
sensor streams.

\section{Dynamical-Systems Operators}\label{sec:attractor}
% ===================================================================== %
\code{attractor\_ops} treats the LTC step \eqref{eq:ltc-affine} as an affine
map $x\mapsto Ax+b$ and reports its convergence behaviour.

\paragraph{Definitions.}
The fixed point solves $(I-A)x^\star=b$, i.e.
$x^\star=(I-A)^{-1}b$ (an error is raised when $I-A$ is singular). The
spectral radius is $\rho(A)=\max_i|\lambda_i(A)|$ and the asymptotic
convergence rate is $-\log\rho(A)$. Given a trajectory with distances
$d_t=\norm{x_t-x^\star}$, the empirical \code{convergence\_rate} is the
negative least-squares slope of $\log d_t$ versus $t$ (over steps with
$d_t>10^{-12}$), and \code{lyapunov\_descent} returns the energy ratios
$V_{t+1}/V_t$ with $V_t=d_t^2$. \code{settling\_time} and \code{basin\_radius}
are bisection/threshold routines and are deliberately piecewise-constant.

\begin{identity}[Fixed point]
For the affine map $x\mapsto Ax+b$ with $I-A$ invertible,
$A x^\star + b = x^\star$. \emph{(Closed form; pinned to \code{atol} $10^{-6}$
over random diagonalisable and symmetric contractions.)}
\end{identity}

\begin{property}[Spectrum and rate]
For $A=P\,\mathrm{diag}(d)\,P^{-1}$, $\rho(A)=\max_i|d_i|$;
\code{is\_contraction}$(A)\Leftrightarrow\rho(A)<1$; and the rate equals
$-\log\rho(A)$. The pair $(\rho,\text{rate})$ is invariant under
permutation similarity $A\mapsto P A P^\top$.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Convergence]
From any start, the relaxation $x_{t+1}=Ax_t+b$ converges to $x^\star$; on
the dominant eigenvector the empirical rate equals the asymptotic rate.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Lyapunov descent for normal maps]
For a \emph{symmetric} contraction with $\rho<1$, every energy ratio
satisfies $V_{t+1}/V_t\le\rho^2$.
\end{property}

\begin{limitation}
The Euclidean-energy descent certificate is sufficient, not necessary. A
\emph{non-normal} contraction ($\rho<1$ but $A^\top A\neq AA^\top$) can grow
$V_t$ transiently while still converging at asymptotic rate $\rho$. Descent
is therefore pinned only for symmetric maps. \code{settling\_time} and
\code{basin\_radius} are non-differentiable, and $-\log\rho$ diverges as
$\rho\to0$ (tests skip $\rho<10^{-3}$).
\end{limitation}

\section{Information-Geometry Operators}\label{sec:geometry}
% ===================================================================== %
\code{geometry\_ops} equips the probability simplex with the Fisher--Rao
metric via the isometry $\phi(p)=\sqrt{p}$ onto the positive orthant of the
unit sphere.

\paragraph{Definitions.}
With the Bhattacharyya coefficient
$BC(p,q)=\sum_i\sqrt{p_iq_i}$, the Fisher--Rao distance is
\begin{equation}
d(p,q)=2\arccos\!\big(\mathrm{clip}(BC(p,q),-1,1)\big)\in[0,\pi].
\end{equation}
The Fisher inner product is $\langle u,v\rangle_p=\sum_i u_iv_i/\max(p_i,\eps)$,
$\eps=10^{-12}$. The geodesic is the pulled-back spherical slerp: with
$u=\sqrt p$, $v=\sqrt q$, $\Omega=\arccos\langle u,v\rangle$,
\begin{equation}
\gamma(t)=\Pi\!\left[\left(
\frac{\sin((1-t)\Omega)\,u+\sin(t\Omega)\,v}{\sin\Omega}\right)^{\!2}\right],
\end{equation}
where $\Pi$ is simplex projection
$\Pi[x]=\max(x,0)/\max(\sum_i\max(x_i,0),\eps)$, with a linear-blend
fallback for $\sin\Omega<10^{-7}$. The exponential and logarithmic maps and
parallel transport are the standard sphere formulas pushed through $\phi$;
\code{geodesic\_barycenter} is the Karcher mean minimising
$\sum_k w_k\,d(b,c_k)^2$.

\begin{property}[Metric axioms]
$d$ is non-negative, symmetric, bounded by $\pi$, zero on the diagonal, and
obeys the triangle inequality; it is invariant under a common permutation of
categories.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Geodesic structure]
$\gamma$ stays on the simplex, satisfies $\gamma(0)=p$, $\gamma(1)=q$, has
constant speed $d(p,\gamma(t))=t\,d(p,q)$, reverses consistently
$\gamma_{p\to q}(t)=\gamma_{q\to p}(1-t)$, and is permutation-equivariant.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Exp/log and transport]
$\log_p(p)=0$; $\log_p(q)$ is zero-sum with $\norm{\log_p(q)}_p=d(p,q)$;
$\exp_p(\log_p(q))=q$; parallel transport preserves tangency and Fisher
norm; the two-point equal-weight barycenter is the geodesic midpoint.
\end{property}

\begin{limitation}
The floor $1/\max(p_i,\eps)$ keeps the metric finite on the simplex
boundary, breaking exactness near zero-probability categories. Because
$\arccos$ has infinite slope at $BC=1$, self-distance is pinned only to
\code{atol} $10^{-6}$. The small-angle geodesic is a linear blend, not the
true arc.
\end{limitation}

\section{Plasticity, Topology, Physics, and Acoustics}
% ===================================================================== %

\subsection{Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (\code{stdp\_ops})}
The asymmetric exponential learning window, with
$\Delta t = t_{\text{post}}-t_{\text{pre}}$, is
\begin{equation}
W(\Delta t)=\begin{cases}
A_+\,e^{-\Delta t/\tau_+}, & \Delta t>0\quad(\text{potentiation}),\\
-A_-\,e^{\Delta t/\tau_-}, & \Delta t<0\quad(\text{depression}),\\
0, & \Delta t=0.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The batch rule \code{pairwise\_stdp} sums $W$ over all pre/post spike pairs;
the online rule \code{stdp\_trace\_update} maintains eligibility traces with
decays $e^{-dt/\tau_\pm}$, ordered decay-then-accumulate so that
simultaneous spikes contribute $0$, matching $W(0)=0$.

\begin{property}[Window shape]
$W$ is causal-signed ($\ge 0$ for $\Delta t>0$, $\le 0$ for $\Delta t<0$),
bounded by $\max(A_+,A_-)$, monotone within each lobe, and antisymmetric
when balanced ($A_+=A_-$, $\tau_+=\tau_-$). It is invariant under a global
time shift, and $\Delta w$ is linear in the amplitudes $A_\pm$.
\end{property}

\begin{property}[Online $=$ batch]\label{prop:stdp-equiv}
For every synapse $(i,j)$, the online trace update's $\Delta w_{ij}$ equals
the batch \code{pairwise\_stdp} computed over that synapse's spike times
(\code{atol} $10^{-9}$). This is the load-bearing equivalence between the
streaming and offline forms.
\end{property}

\begin{limitation}
The module provides the plasticity \emph{rule} only; gating L2 memory writes
on salience ignitions is a separate, not-yet-implemented integration. The
trace form requires a uniform $dt$ grid.
\end{limitation}

\subsection{$H_0$ Persistent Homology (\code{topology\_ops})}
The module rests on a classical fact: single-linkage clustering equals the
minimum spanning tree (MST), and the multiset of $H_0$ death scales equals
the multiset of MST edge weights, so
$\text{Betti-}0(\eps)=N-\#\{\text{MST edges}\le\eps\}$.

\begin{identity}[MST $=$ $H_0$]
The barcode death multiset equals the MST edge-weight multiset; the MST has
exactly $N-1$ edges and \code{total\_persistence} equals their sum.
\emph{(Closed form; pinned over well-separated clouds.)}
\end{identity}

\begin{property}[Betti curve and SRTD]
Betti-$0$ equals $N$ at scale $0$, equals $1$ at large scale, and is
monotone non-increasing in $\eps$. \code{total\_persistence} is permutation-
and translation-invariant and scales linearly with the cloud. The sorted
death-distance surrogate
$\mathrm{SRTD}_p(X,Y)=\big(\tfrac1n\sum_k|s^X_k-s^Y_k|^p\big)^{1/p}$ (sorted,
zero-padded deaths) is symmetric, non-negative, zero on coincidence,
permutation-invariant, and obeys the triangle inequality for equal-size
clouds.
\end{property}

\begin{limitation}
This is $H_0$ only -- connected components, the cheap exact part. Loops
($H_1$), voids ($H_2$), and certified bottleneck/RTD distances are out of
scope; SRTD is a regulariser \emph{signal}, not a certified metric. Integer
Betti numbers are non-differentiable; only the metric quantities are.
\end{limitation}

\subsection{Newtonian Micro-Physics (\code{physics\_ops})}
Semi-implicit (symplectic) Euler updates velocity first,
$v'=v+a\,dt$, then $x'=x+v'\,dt$; velocity Verlet uses the kick--drift--kick
form. Plummer-softened gravity is
$a_i=G\sum_{j\neq i} m_j (p_j-p_i)/(\norm{p_j-p_i}^2+\delta^2)^{3/2}$
with $\delta=10^{-3}$. Collisions apply the standard restitution impulse to
approaching overlapping pairs.

\begin{identity}[Symplectic closed form]
Under constant acceleration $a$, semi-implicit Euler gives exactly
$v_k=v_0+k\,dt\,a$ and
$x_k=x_0+k\,dt\,v_0+a\,dt^2\,\tfrac{k(k+1)}{2}$.
\end{identity}

\begin{property}[Conservation laws]
Pairwise gravity obeys $\sum_i m_i a_i=0$ (Newton's third law) and is
translation-invariant. Collisions conserve momentum unconditionally; elastic
collisions conserve kinetic energy; inelastic collisions never create energy
($KE'\le KE+10^{-9}$ for any restitution $e\in[0,1]$). A force-free rollout
is a straight line at constant energy, and a closed $N$-body rollout
conserves total momentum at every snapshot.
\end{property}

\begin{limitation}
Integration is first-order symplectic: energy-\emph{stable}, not exact.
Verlet assumes a position-only force and degrades for velocity-dependent
forces. Simultaneous multi-body contacts are resolved in one pass --
exact for isolated pairs, approximate for dense pile-ups. Contact/wall
thresholds are non-differentiable.
\end{limitation}

\subsection{Binaural Acoustics (\code{acoustic\_ops})}
With sound speed $c=343$, propagation delay is $\norm{s-r}/c$, spreading
gain is $\text{ref}/\max(r,\eps)$, the interaural time difference is
$\text{ITD}=\text{delay}_L-\text{delay}_R$, and the level difference is
$\text{ILD}=20\log_{10}(r_L/r_R)$ dB. Localisation inverts the far-field
model, $\theta=\arcsin(\mathrm{clip}(\text{ITD}\cdot c/\text{head\_width},-1,1))$.

\begin{property}[Cue consistency]
Delay recovers distance and scales as $1/c$; gain recovers \code{ref\_dist}
and decreases monotonically; ITD is antisymmetric under ear swap, bounded by
$\text{head\_width}/c$, and vanishes on the median plane; ITD and ILD are
sign-aligned; Doppler raises pitch on approach and lowers it on recession;
superposition is linear and obeys the triangle inequality in magnitude; and
localisation inverts the far-field model exactly, saturating at the poles.
The composed \code{binaural\_scene} agrees field-by-field with the
individual cue operators.
\end{property}

\begin{limitation}
The ITD$\to$azimuth map is the far-field plane-wave model: exact far from the
head, approximate near it. The Doppler denominator is clamped away from the
shock-wave regime. This is not a learned head-related transfer function.
\end{limitation}

\section{Spatial and Ingest Operators}\label{sec:ingest}
% ===================================================================== %

\subsection{Spatial Graphs (\code{spatial\_ops})}
Pairwise distance is $d_{ij}=\sqrt{\norm{p_i-p_j}^2+\eps}$; unit relative
directions, 2-D bearings, radius and $k$-NN graphs, reachability (transitive
closure), hop distance (BFS), and connected components (min-index label
propagation) complete the toolkit.

\begin{property}[Geometric and graph laws]
Distance is a metric (symmetric, zero-diagonal, triangle inequality) and is
invariant under rigid motion. Radius graphs are symmetric with edges exactly
at the threshold and monotone in radius; $k$-NN graphs are symmetric with
degree $\ge k$. Reachability contains the seeds, is monotone in the hop
budget, and agrees with finite hop distance; component labels are constant
along edges and identify mutually reachable nodes.
\end{property}

\subsection{Stream Alignment (\code{ingest\_ops})}
Sensor streams arriving on an irregular clock are resampled onto the LTC
grid. Linear resampling clamps queries into the sample span (never
extrapolating) and interpolates
$\text{out}=v_0+w(v_1-v_0)$, $w=\mathrm{clip}((q-t_0)/\max(t_1-t_0,\eps),0,1)$;
a previous-sample (zero-order-hold) mode is also provided. Coverage flags
$\text{gap}\le\text{max\_gap}$ (default $1.5\,dt$).

\begin{property}[Exactness on affine signals]
Linear resampling is \emph{exact} on any affine signal $a\,t+b$ over any
input clock (\code{atol} $10^{-7}$) and never leaves the convex hull of the
samples. Zero-order-hold emits only real sample values and holds the most
recent past sample. Gaps are non-negative and vanish exactly at sample
times; coverage is monotone in \code{max\_gap}; jitter is zero on a uniform
clock; and \code{align\_stream} equals resampling and coverage applied
separately.
\end{property}

\begin{limitation}
Resampling is exact only to its model order (affine for the linear mode);
across a long gap it flags the gap and defers to the predictor rather than
inventing a high-order fit. Coverage thresholds are non-differentiable.
There is no Kalman/learned filtering.
\end{limitation}

\section{Memory, Continual Evaluation, and Salience}\label{sec:memory}
% ===================================================================== %

\subsection{Experience Replay (\code{replay.py})}
\code{ReservoirBuffer} implements Vitter's Algorithm~R for a uniform sample
of an unbounded stream in $O(C)$ memory and $O(1)$ time per item: with
capacity $C$ and 0-based index $i$, item $i$ fills slot $i$ while $i<C$;
otherwise a draw $j\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\{0,\dots,i\}$ overwrites slot $j$
when $j<C$. The RNG is an owned, seeded CPU generator, so draws are
device-independent and reproducible. \code{ClassBalancedReservoir} runs
$\lfloor C/n_{\text{classes}}\rfloor$ slots per class.

\begin{property}[Algorithm R]
After $n>C$ items each is retained with empirical frequency $\approx C/n$;
over $4000$ Monte-Carlo runs ($C=10$, $n=200$) the max$-$min retention
frequency is below $0.05$ and the first-versus-last difference below $0.04$.
Draws are seed-deterministic; \code{add\_batch} matches repeated \code{add};
\code{reset()} clears the buffer, schema, and RNG; the class-balanced variant
splits its budget evenly regardless of arrival skew.
\end{property}

\begin{limitation}
The uniformity guarantee is statistical -- pinned via Monte-Carlo frequency
bands, not an algebraic identity. Class-balanced capacity is
$\lfloor C/n_{\text{classes}}\rfloor\cdot n_{\text{classes}}$; remainder
slots are deliberately unused for fairness.
\end{limitation}

\subsection{Continual-Learning Metrics (\code{continual\_eval.py})}
Given the accuracy matrix $R$ with $R[i,j]$ the accuracy on task $j$ after
training through task $i$, the metrics (Lopez-Paz \& Ranzato 2017; Chaudhry
et al.\ 2018), all computed in \code{float64}, are
\begin{align}
\text{ACC} &= \tfrac1T\textstyle\sum_j R[T{-}1,j], &
\text{LA} &= \tfrac1T\textstyle\sum_i R[i,i],\\
\text{BWT} &= \tfrac1{T-1}\textstyle\sum_{i<T-1}\!\big(R[T{-}1,i]-R[i,i]\big), &
\text{FWT} &= \tfrac1{T-1}\textstyle\sum_{i\ge1}\!\big(R[i{-}1,i]-b_i\big),\\
\text{FM} &= \tfrac1{T-1}\textstyle\sum_{i<T-1}\!\Big(\max_{i\le l<T-1}R[l,i]-R[T{-}1,i]\Big).
\end{align}

\begin{property}[Metric correctness]
Perfect retention gives $\text{BWT}=\text{FM}=0$; total forgetting gives
$\text{BWT}=-1$, $\text{FM}=1$; the formulas match hand-computed matrices;
single-task inputs collapse to the identity/zero cases; and non-square,
non-2-D, empty, or mismatched-baseline inputs are rejected.
\end{property}

\begin{limitation}\label{lim:fm}
The implementation defines forgetting as
$\max_{i\le l<T-1}R[l,i]-R[T{-}1,i]$ (best-ever minus final). The module's
prose claims this is always $\ge 0$, but under positive backward transfer
the best old-task accuracy can be the just-learned value while the final
exceeds it, making FM \emph{negative} -- the tests pin a case at $-0.2$. We
report the code's actual behaviour, not the prose claim. Metrics read only
the entries they are defined on, so un-evaluated future-task entries may be
\code{NaN} without corrupting any score.
\end{limitation}

\subsection{Salience / Ignition Detection (\code{salience\_events.py})}
A streaming Schmitt-trigger finite-state machine flags global-workspace
ignitions. With EMA mean $\mu$ and second moment $\overline{x^2}$ (decay
$d$), the standard deviation is
$\sigma=\sqrt{\max(\overline{x^2}-\mu^2,\eps)}$, $\eps=10^{-6}$, and the
one-sided surprise is $z=(x-\mu)/\sigma$. The machine ignites when
$z\ge\text{ignite\_z}$ and quiesces when $z\le\text{release\_z}$ (with
$\text{release\_z}<\text{ignite\_z}$), with a refractory countdown after
each event. The baseline adapts only on event-free, non-refractory samples,
so the detector re-arms on sustained shifts rather than latching.

\begin{property}[Detector contract]
The detector is deterministic, and the online \code{update} path equals the
batch \code{observe} path. No event fires during warmup; events strictly
alternate ignition$\to$quiescence starting with an ignition; consecutive
events are separated by at least $\text{refractory}+1$ steps; every ignition
carries $z\ge\text{ignite\_z}$ and every quiescence $z\le\text{release\_z}$.
A constant stream never fires; a single spike on a settled baseline fires
exactly one ignition. The event stream is invariant under an additive DC
offset $x\mapsto x+b$ (pinned to floating-point precision,
$|z_0-z_1|\le 10^{-9}\max(1,|z_0|)$).
\end{property}

\begin{limitation}
Because the variance floor $\eps$ is absolute, not relative, the detector is
\emph{not} invariant under a multiplicative rescale $x\mapsto a\,x$ near a
flat baseline; only additive-shift invariance is pinned. Salience is
one-sided (upward departures only).
\end{limitation}

\section{On the Meaning of ``Proven''}\label{sec:honesty}
% ===================================================================== %
We separate three grades of claim, in decreasing order of strength.

\paragraph{(1) Closed-form identities.} A handful of statements are exact
algebra and would survive a hand proof: the affine fixed point
$x^\star=(I-A)^{-1}b$; the MST/$H_0$ death-scale equivalence; the
constant-acceleration symplectic-Euler closed form; the exactness of linear
resampling on affine signals; and Vitter's $C/n$ retention identity
(probabilistic but exact in expectation). These we label
\emph{Identity}.

\paragraph{(2) Property-test-pinned laws.} The majority of statements above
are verified by Hypothesis property tests: each draws inputs from a bounded,
\code{assume()}-filtered space (typically $80$ or $120$ examples) and asserts
an invariant within a finite tolerance. This is strong, reproducible,
regression-guarding evidence -- the invariants have survived a directed
adversarial search -- but it is \emph{not} a formal, machine-checked proof
over the entire input domain. We label these \emph{Property} and we
intend the word literally: the law is pinned, not proven.

\paragraph{(3) Statistical / scenario claims.} Two operators are pinned by
hand-computed unit tests rather than property tests (\code{replay} and
\code{continual\_eval}); the replay uniformity guarantee in particular is
Monte-Carlo statistical, asserted via frequency bands. These are the weakest
form of evidence in the layer and are flagged as such.

This taxonomy is the paper's central honesty commitment. Where prose in the
codebase over-claimed -- notably the ``$\text{FM}\ge 0$'' assertion
contradicted by its own test (Limitation~\ref{lim:fm}) -- we have reported
the code's true behaviour.

\section{Limitations and Non-Goals}\label{sec:limitations}
% ===================================================================== %
Collecting the per-module caveats, the layer's honest boundaries are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Epsilon floors break exact scale-invariance.} The Fisher
metric's $1/\max(p,\eps)$ and the salience detector's absolute variance
floor make those operators only \emph{additively} invariant, not
multiplicatively, near degenerate inputs.
\item \textbf{Approximate physics.} Integration is first-order
symplectic (energy-stable, not exact); dense simultaneous contacts are
resolved approximately.
\item \textbf{$H_0$-only topology.} Loops and voids are out of scope; SRTD
is a surrogate signal, not a certified bottleneck distance.
\item \textbf{Far-field acoustics.} Localisation is exact only in the
far field.
\item \textbf{Non-differentiable thresholds.} Graph construction, settling
time, basin radius, coverage masks, and integer Betti numbers are
piecewise-constant and cannot pass gradients.
\item \textbf{Unintegrated by design.} The plasticity and Riemannian
operators are intentionally kept at the operator layer, available but not
wired into the training objective.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
% ===================================================================== %
The MT-LNN operator layer is a small algebra of brain-inspired primitives
that earns its composability from a strict contract: backbone-decoupled,
zero-parameter, resettable, dtype-disciplined, and property-pinned. We have
given exact definitions for all eleven operators and the LTC core they
instrument, stated the invariants their tests pin, and -- crucially --
separated the few closed-form identities from the larger body of
property-test-pinned laws and the handful of statistical guarantees. The
result is not a benchmark claim but a methodology: brain-inspired mechanisms
become trustworthy when each is distilled into a parameter-free operator
whose every law is an executable, honestly-graded test.
